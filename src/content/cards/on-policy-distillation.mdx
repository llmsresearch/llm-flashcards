On-policy distillation trains a student using sequences the current student generates. The teacher provides feedback on those sequences, including prefixes the student would encounter when producing answers on its own.

## Why the generated prefix matters

With a fixed teacher dataset, the student mostly trains on the teacher's continuations. At inference time, one different student token can lead to a prefix that was never in that dataset. The next prediction must then be made from a different state.

In the card's loop, the student samples a continuation. The teacher evaluates the same prefix, and a distillation loss encourages the student to match the teacher's guidance there. Training updates the student, then later rollouts reflect its changed behavior. The teacher remains fixed in this example.

## The tradeoff

The training data follows the student's current behavior, but teacher evaluation and fresh generation add cost. Implementations differ in their divergence objective, feedback representation and mixture of on-policy and fixed data.

Do not confuse this with ordinary supervised training on saved teacher answers. The defining feature here is who generated the states being trained on: the current student.
