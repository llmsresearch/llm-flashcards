pass@k and pass^k ask different questions about repeated attempts. pass@k asks whether at least one of k attempts succeeds. pass^k asks whether all k attempts succeed. The first measures a chance to find a solution; the second measures repeatability.

## One task, three independent attempts

For an illustrative task where every independent attempt has success probability 0.8:

```text
pass@3 = 1 - (1 - 0.8)^3 = 0.992
pass^3 = 0.8^3 = 0.512
```

At least one success is very likely, while getting three successes is much less likely. The attempts are fresh runs of the same task, not three steps within one agent run.

## Report the experiment, not just the label

The formulas above assume independent attempts and a fixed success probability for this one task. Measured benchmarks may estimate their metrics from finite samples. Task difficulty also varies, so compute the appropriate per-task quantity before aggregating; raising one pooled success rate to a power can give a different answer.

For pass@k to describe a useful workflow, the system also needs a way to identify a successful candidate. Producing one correct answer somewhere among several attempts does not mean the user can reliably select it.
